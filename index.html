Who Is This For?

We built the Moab Men's Retreat for Christian men who want real adventure and to grow spiritually at the same time. You'll come away with a deeper knowledge of yourself, other men, and the Lord. It's open to guys 22 or older. You don't need any off-road experience — we love first-timers just as much as the guys who've been wheeling for years. In fact, if you've got your own rig, bring it along.

Two things to be straight about.

First

Our faith is the backbone of the whole trip: we believe Jesus is the Son of God, both fully man and fully God, and the only way of salvation — so we're looking for men who believe the same.

Second

This is a retreat, not counseling. If you're going through a personal crisis or need real help right now, seek out the support you need from a professional. You'll be in a better place for the next trip.

Straight answer

Will It Be Cold in November?

Moab winters are dry and cold — morning temperatures in the 20s and 30s, so an open-air UTV ride would be an endurance test a lot of guys don't want. That's why we'll have fully enclosed Jeeps with heat and air to keep the cabin at the perfect temp so our winter tour becomes an adventure in Moab, not an adventure in freezing.

Pack smart

What to Bring

November in Moab means cold mornings, mild afternoons, and nights that drop into the 30s°F. Layers are the whole game.

Clothing

  • Layers: base layer, insulating mid-layer, and a wind/waterproof outer shell
  • Warm jacket and a hat or cap for the evening fire
  • Sturdy closed-toe shoes or boots for the trail; a second casual pair for the house
  • Gloves — for cold mornings and rocky handholds
  • Swim gear — the house has a hot tub and an above-ground pool

Gear & Documents

  • Utah OHV Education Course completion card — required for every driver
  • Driver's license
  • Sunglasses and sunscreen — the high-desert sun is strong even in November
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Personal medications and any signed waiver Dave has sent you
  • Camera or phone — the views are worth it
  • Trip insurance (recommended — add it on your own before you travel)

What Not to Bring

  • Alcohol and smoking are not allowed at any point on the trip. Leave those at home.
  • Anything you're not willing to get dusty. The trail and the side-by-sides are dirty work.
Required Training

Every driver must complete the Utah OHV Education Course online before arrival. It's free but required — bring your completion card with you.
recreation.utah.gov/ohv-education

A word from the trail lead

An Invitation to Brotherhood

Jason Wells · Trail Lead & Host

"Men — when was the last time you had a group of brothers who genuinely saw you, believed in you, and you knew they had your six?"

Not the guys you nod to at church. Not your coworkers, your coaching buddies, the guys in the group chat who only talk about the game.

Brothers. The kind who'd notice if you went quiet. The kind you could call at 2am and they'd answer. The kind who see past what you do for everyone else and actually know you.

If you're honest — it's been a while. Maybe it's never really happened.

You've got people who need you. Depend on you. Lean on you.

But who's standing back to back with you?

It's time to air down, drop it in 4Lo, and engage your lockers — together.

This is not your typical church service, or a paved road to a prairie. This is real life. Raw and unfiltered. And it calls for true faith.

Because the enemy is coming for your fire, your family, your soul. There's a target placed directly on every man's back — but our culture, and even the church, has been training us to fight this war alone, and with the wrong weapons.

The Old Way vs. The True Way

You have been taught that a man must carry it all alone.

But I tell you — brothers who journey together break chains and live free.

You have heard it said you must man up and push through.

But I tell you — those who wait upon the Lord shall renew their strength.

You have heard it said being a man means never being weak.

But I tell you — God's power is made perfect in our weakness.

You have heard it said silence makes you strong.

But I tell you — confession heals and makes you unbreakable.

You have heard it said never depend on anyone.

But I tell you — the man who depends on the Lord will never be forsaken, and a cord of three strands shall not be broken.

You have heard it said endure by pretending nothing hurts.

But I tell you — pain is where God shapes warriors.

The Road Ahead

Life's obstacles are coming — no bypass, no detour. You will be challenged. Bruised. Tested.

And you have a choice.

Enough traversing this trail alone.

Go it alone and endure or go together and thrive.
Keep powering through in your own strength or wait upon the Lord and renew your strength.
Deny the pain or embrace your pain and let it fuel your fire.

This is how men are forged. How the weak become strong. How men lead.

Leave the slickrock refueled. Reignited. Unstoppable.
